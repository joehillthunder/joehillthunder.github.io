Why it works in 3D

An isometric camera never moves closer or further away, so the whole scene sits in one shallow box of depth. That's exactly the range glasses-free panels show most sharply and comfortably. Walls, pillars and stairs read at a glance, and you can judge distances to enemies and ground effects.

Loot beams, spell rings and torchlight show it off best: vertical light rising toward you over a floor that recedes behind the glass.

Try the scene above: tap a demon to smite it, tap the floor to move, and press Cast Frost Nova when they crowd in. Orange beams are legendaries.

Play the real game in 3D

  1. You need an Acer SpatialLabs laptop or a SpatialLabs View display. TrueGame doesn't run on other screens.
  2. Install Diablo III through Battle.net, then open the SpatialLabs TrueGame app and find Diablo III in its list.
  3. Pick the 3D Ultra profile and launch the game from TrueGame so the profile is applied.
  4. The camera is fixed, so you can push depth further here than in a third-person game. Raise it until the floor feels like a tabletop.

Wanted Diablo II? Diablo II: Resurrected has no TrueGame or Odyssey 3D profile as of this snapshot. Diablo III is the one to play in 3D for now. We'll add D2R here if a profile appears.
