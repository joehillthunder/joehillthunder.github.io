Why it works in 3D

Open worlds come alive in stereo when they have layers, and Elden Ring is all layers: grass and gravestones near you, your character in the middle, castles and the golden tree far behind. The third-person camera keeps your character at a steady depth, so your eyes settle on them and the world opens out behind.

Particles carry the effect: golden leaves, embers, spell glows and the motes rising off a site of grace all drift through the space in front of the scenery.

Try the scene above: tap the site of grace to rest, tap the knight to swing, or switch to night. The leaves start about 6 cm behind the glass and end about 6 cm in front.

Play the real game in 3D

  1. You need a Leia SR display with the SR platform installed. This game's SR profile ships in Acer's launcher, so for now that means an Acer SpatialLabs laptop or SpatialLabs View display.
  2. Install Elden Ring on Steam, then open SpatialLabs TrueGame, Acer's SR launcher, and find Elden Ring in its list.
  3. Pick the SR stereo profile (Acer calls it 3D Ultra) and launch from there, so the SR runtime renders and weaves both eyes.
  4. It's a demanding game rendered twice per frame. Use a lower preset than in 2D so boss fights stay smooth, and pick moderate depth for long sessions.
