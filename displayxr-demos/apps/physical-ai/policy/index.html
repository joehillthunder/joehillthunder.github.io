How this stand-in works, and what it isn't

The policy is a small network (15 inputs, two 128-unit layers) trained offline on – minutes of scripted demonstrations with both containers, then refined with DAgger. It is goal-conditioned: it reaches for whatever goal it is given. The prompt reader takes the order of grasps and the first drop point from the demo and turns them into those goals. No weights change.

This is a small stand-in for a robot foundation model, made to show the workflow on a 3D display. Real foundation models learn far more from a short demonstration than this one does.

The kitchen and SO-101 arm come from Lightwheel's open-source LeIsaac assets (Apache-2.0).

Why a fleet view matters

See what your robots learn, in glasses-free 3D

Robot foundation models are moving toward learning a task from a short demonstration and working across grippers and hands. Every step of that loop is spatial: the demonstration, the policy's grasp, a fleet at work. A glasses-free 3D display shows it in real depth, with no headset and at a desk.
